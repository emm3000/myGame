# CONTEXT.md

Glossary of the game's domain. One line per term, the term as code and docs use it, and the OGame concept it replaces where one exists. Names a player sees come from `docs/setting/` once it exists and from `docs/lore/` until then, and enter here second; the shipped Spanish labels are in `docs/lore/names.md`. Decisions behind the terms: `docs/adr/`.

ADR 035 moves the game from medieval Vadoalto to Townword, a present-day town, and supersedes the medieval terms below. The code still uses them, so their entries stand until the town rebuild's tickets rename them; *Superseded by ADR 035* records each mapping, and *Learning* holds the new terms.

## Holdings

- **Player** — an account; holds one fief or two. Identified by `PlayerId`.
- **Fief** — the holding a player develops: land, buildings, stores, peasants, each fief with its own arts, units, slots and chronicle. Replaces *planet*. At most two per lord, the content's cap: the first given at sign-up, the second founded as new land. Units never move between the two, and resources only as the cargo of a transport (ADR 023, ADR 024).
- **Coordinates** — `kingdom:province:plot`, the fief's place on the map. A lord's first fief takes the lowest plot of the first kingdom that is neither held nor reserved; a second stands on the plot its founding was sent to (ADR 023). Replaces *galaxy:system:position*.
- **Kingdom** — the great region a fief lies in, a number in the data and a name on screen (Vadoalto is kingdom 1). Provinces and plots inside it are numbered. Replaces *galaxy*.
- **Province** — a numbered region of a kingdom with `plotsPerProvince` plots and one terrain, set by its number and shared by every plot in it. Replaces *system*.
- **Plot** — the numbered place in a province that one fief holds; free until a fief is founded on it, held after. A free plot is **reserved** while a founding is on the road to it, from the dispatch until the founding is recalled or applied: no march is sent to it and no first fief is placed on it (ADR 023). Replaces *position*.
- **Terrain** — what a province is made of: lowlands, uplands or ridges. It adds to the rate of the resource it favours, on top of the base rate and the producers, and forbids nothing.
- **Kingdom map** — the view of one province at a time: its terrain and every plot with the name of the fief holding it, each of the viewer's own marked. Browsable within the viewer's kingdom from province 1 to `lastProvince`, the last province holding a fief plus one; a province outside that range is refused `ProvinceNotFound` (ADR 014). It shows each camp on a free plot with its tier and its strength at the read instant (ADR 020), and a reserved plot as reserved, the viewer's own founding marked and no other founder named (ADR 023). Read to plan, and four orders alone are given from it: a forage march to a free plot without a camp, an attack on a camp's plot and a founding on a free plot without a camp, never to a held plot, a reserved one nor any of the viewer's own, and a transport to the viewer's other fief, never to the fief the map is read from nor to another lord's (ADR 019, ADR 020, ADR 023, ADR 024). Replaces *galaxy view*.
- **New land** — a lord's second fief, founded and never taken: one settler marches alone from the lord's fief to a free plot without a camp in the same kingdom, the lord naming the new fief at dispatch. The plot is reserved while the settler is on the road; at the arrival, applied on the next read of the fief the settler left, the fief is founded there with the stocks a first fief starts with, nothing built, no unit and no art, accruing from the arrival, and the settler leaves the count of the fief it left. A plot held by then founds nothing and the settler turns home. A founding still on the road counts toward the cap (ADR 023). Replaces *colony*.

## Resources

- **Resource** — one of wood, stone, iron, gold, food. Each has an amount, a rate per hour and a capacity on a fief.
- **Wood / Stone / Iron** — the building materials, produced by the sawmill, the quarry and the iron mine. Replace *metal / crystal / deuterium*.
- **Gold** — the scarce currency for upkeep and trade, accruing from the fief's tolls and tithes at its base rate; no building produces it. Replaces the premium resource but is earned in play.
- **Food** — a stored resource like the others, produced by farms and spent on building costs. It is not eaten down over time in this phase.
- **Peasants** — the workforce. The fief supplies a base number and each farm level adds more; each building level and each unit occupies some, and an open recruit order occupies its units' peasants from the instant it is placed, delivered or not (ADR 018). Free peasants = supplied − occupied, a derived number that never grows on its own. An upgrade charges only the increase in occupancy against the free peasants, releasing the current level's occupancy. Replaces *energy*.
- **Warehouse** — the building that sets a resource's capacity.
- **Base rate** — the rate per hour every fief earns of each resource from founding, with no building; a producer's rate and the terrain bonus add on top of it.
- **Accrual** — the amount a resource holds at a later instant, computed on read segment by segment: the span since the stored instant is split at every season boundary it crosses, and each segment accrues at the rates in force at its start as `max(amount, min(capacity, amount + rate × elapsed))`, floored on its own. A stock under the capacity fills up to it, a stock above it (after a refund) freezes, neither accruing nor clamped (ADR 005, ADR 016).

## Buildings

- **Building** — a structure on a fief with a level; each level has a cost, a build duration and an effect.
- **Sawmill / Quarry / Iron mine / Farm** — the producers.
- **Library** — the building where arts are studied, one at a time in its study slot. Its built level, never the projected one, gates which art levels can be studied and shortens every study. Replaces *research lab*.
- **Barracks** — the seventh building, where units are recruited, one order at a time in its recruit slot. Its built level, never the projected one, gates recruiting, each unit kind from the level content gives it, and shortens every unit's training, as the library's shortens a study; it holds no rate (ADR 018, ADR 021). Replaces *shipyard*.
- **Build slot** — the single place on a fief where the upgrade in progress builds; one upgrade at a time. A slot with an upgrade in progress is **busy**.
- **Build queue** — the ordered upgrades waiting behind the busy slot, at most the content cap. Each entry keeps the building, the target level, the cost it debited and the duration fixed at its enqueue, and starts the instant the one before it finishes.
- **Enqueue** — the single atomic mutation that debits resources and either starts an upgrade in the idle slot or appends it to the build queue. The target level and the peasants are judged on the fief as it will stand once the slot and the queue finish. Staffs the upgrade by the delta: it releases the current level's occupancy and charges only the increase against the free peasants, so a level-2 upgrade of a building already at level 1 needs `occupancy(2) − occupancy(1)` free peasants, not `occupancy(2)`. Refused with a named reason when the build queue is full, resources are short or free peasants are too few.
- **Resolve** — applying the finished upgrades, the finished study, the ended recruit order, the fought battle, the arrived founding, the returned march and the arrived cargo to the fief, on read, in the order they finished: each stretch accrues to the next finish at the rates in force, set by the building and art levels before it, then that finish applies. A finished upgrade raises its building and the next waiting upgrade starts at that instant; a finished study raises its art and leaves the study slot idle; an ended recruit order adds its units to the count and leaves the recruit slot idle, while an order still delivering is left untouched (ADR 018); an attack at its arrival fights its battle, the dead leaving the count then, and keeps the march with the survivors when won or leaves the march slot idle when lost (ADR 020); a founding at its arrival founds the new fief, takes its settler out of the count and leaves the march slot idle, or turns the settler home when the plot is held by then (ADR 023); a returned march credits its loot to the stocks even above the capacity, where they freeze, and leaves the march slot idle, while a march still away is left untouched (ADR 019); an arrived cargo is credited to the stocks of the fief it was sent to, even above the capacity, and is no longer on its way (ADR 024). Works that finish at one instant all apply there, the upgrade first, then the study, then the order, then the battle, then the founding, then the march, then the cargo (ADR 024). A build queue left behind an idle build slot, which only a cancel stored before #92 left, restarts at the stored instant, the cancel instant. Every waiting upgrade that no longer fits the fief as projected (a level whose lower level was cancelled, too few free peasants) is dropped first, with a full refund.
- **Cancel** — the single atomic mutation that removes one entry, named by its building and target level, which no other entry of the slot or the queue shares: the upgrade in the busy slot or a waiting entry of the build queue. It refunds 100 % of the cost the entry stored at the enqueue, added to the stocks at the cancel instant even above the capacity, where they freeze. Cancelling the upgrade in the slot starts the next waiting entry at the cancel instant, or leaves the slot idle; cancelling a waiting entry closes the gap, so the entries after it keep their order and finish earlier. Then it cancels in **cascade**: the remaining entries are walked in order on the projection, and every one that no longer fits (its target level is not the projected level plus one, or the projected free peasants cannot staff its increase) is cancelled too, with its own full refund. Every finished upgrade is resolved first; an entry that has finished by then, or that the slot and the queue no longer hold, is refused, never swapped for a neighbour. It touches no peasant count, since occupancy derives from built levels. Cancelling the **study** in the study slot, named by its art and target level, refunds 100 % of the cost the study stored at its start the same way, even above the capacity; there is no cascade, since the study slot has no queue, and the build slot and the build queue stay untouched. A study that has finished by then is applied first and refused, never cancelled. Cancelling the **recruit order** in the recruit slot, named by its unit and its start, keeps the units delivered by the cancel instant, a delivery at that instant counting, and refunds 100 % of the stored cost of the undelivered units the same way, even above the capacity, freeing their peasants; there is no cascade, and the build slot, the build queue and the study slot stay untouched. An order that has ended by then is applied first and refused, never cancelled.

## Knowledge and arms

- **Art** — craft knowledge a fief holds at a level; each level multiplies one resource rate by the content's percent, on top of base, producers and terrain. It occupies no peasants. Replaces *technology*.
- **Smithing** — the art that raises the iron rate.
- **Masonry** — the art that raises the stone rate.
- **Study** — the timed act that raises an art one level, paid in materials and gold at its start; the library's built level gates it and shortens it. Cancelled with a full refund, like an upgrade, until it finishes.
- **Study slot** — the library's single place for the study in progress; one study at a time, no queue. A slot with a study in progress is **busy**.
- **Unit** — a count per kind the fief holds, of infantry, cavalry, archers and settlers; rams come later (ADR 021, ADR 023, ADR 025). The **archer** is the fighting kind that falls last in a won battle, with an infantry's strength and pace and half its carry (ADR 025). The **settler** is the kind that founds new land: the dearest kind and the one that needs the highest barracks level, of strength 0 and carry 0, taken by no forage, no attack and no transport and sent alone on a founding (ADR 023, ADR 024). Content gives each kind its cost, its training, its peasants, its strength, its carry, its pace on the road and the barracks level it needs. Each unit occupies the peasants content gives it, as a building level does, and eats nothing. The count a read answers is the stored count plus the units the open recruit order has delivered by then (ADR 018). A unit is **at home** or **away**: the units away are the march's count of each kind, still counted and still occupying their peasants; the units at home are each kind's count less them, and only they can be sent (ADR 019, ADR 021). The dead of a battle leave their kind's count at the instant of the battle and free their peasants; the march then holds the survivors (ADR 020). A settler that founds leaves the count at its arrival and frees its peasants the same way (ADR 023). Replaces *ships*.
- **Recruit order** — the timed act that adds N units of one kind to the fief, paid in full and staffed in full the instant it is placed, against the built barracks level. It delivers one unit every per-unit duration fixed at the order with the season in force then, never changed, derived on read and never stored, and closes at its last delivery, when its units move into the count. Bounded by the stocks and by the lesser of the free and the projected free peasants; cancelled until its last delivery, keeping the delivered units (ADR 018).
- **Recruit slot** — the barracks' single place for the recruit order in progress; one order at a time, no queue. A slot with an order in progress is **busy** (ADR 018).
- **Army** — units on a march that meets another lord: scouting, PvP combat. The party of a forage march or of an attack on a camp is the yard's errand, not an army, as the lore settled it (#293, ADR 020). Replaces *fleet*. Not in this phase (W1).
- **March** — a party of the fief's units on the road to a plot of its kingdom and back, sent from the map, carrying a count per kind of the units at home (ADR 021), under one of four **orders**: a forage, with a stay of whole hours within the content's longest, an attack on the plot's camp, with no stay (ADR 020), a **founding**, one settler alone to a free plot without a camp, with no stay and no way back unless it is recalled or the plot is held at its arrival (ADR 023), or a **transport**, infantry, cavalry and archers carrying a cargo to the lord's other fief, with no stay (ADR 024, ADR 025). A forage, an attack and a founding go to a free plot; a transport goes to a held one, the lord's own. Its road time one way is the provinces crossed and the plots crossed, each at the content's seconds, scaled by the road percent of the slowest kind sent and by the road percent of the season in force at dispatch, rounded up once, the same back (ADR 022); it passes three phases derived on read from its departure, its road time and its stay, **outbound** until the arrival, **foraging** until the stay ends, **returning** until the return, an instant on a boundary reading the later one. The plot is checked free and not reserved at dispatch; a forage and an attack neither reserve nor deplete it, and a founding reserves it (ADR 023). No forage, attack or founding is sent to a fief of the sending lord, and a transport is sent to the lord's other fief alone, never to the fief it leaves (ADR 024). A forage march meets no one, an attack meets the bandits of its camp and no one else, and a founding and a transport meet no one. A season reaches a march at dispatch alone, through its road and its forage loot, never its stay, its battle or an attack's loot, and a season that turns while the march is away changes nothing of it (ADR 022). An attack has no foraging phase: it turns back at the arrival, when its battle is fought. A transport has no foraging phase either: it turns back at the arrival, when its cargo is no longer the party's, and returns empty (ADR 024). A founding ends at its arrival, when the fief is founded and the settler stays; recalled, or turned home at a held plot, it ends at the return as every other march does. A march ends at the return, when the resolve credits its loot, its return comes sooner if the lord recalls it (ADR 019, ADR 020). Replaces *mission*.
- **Party** — the count of each unit kind a march takes: whole numbers from 0 and at least one unit in all. It keeps the pace of its slowest kind, carries the sum of its units' carries and fights with the sum of their strengths (ADR 021). The party of a forage, of an attack or of a transport holds no settler, and the party of a founding is one settler and nothing else (ADR 023, ADR 024).
- **Forage** — what a march does at its plot: it takes what the province's terrain yields, lowlands food and wood, uplands wood and stone, ridges stone and iron, never gold, at the content's rate per unit and hour, whatever its kind (ADR 019, ADR 021). The season in force at dispatch scales what it takes of each resource by the percent that scales the fief's rate of it, so a forage follows the harvest: more food in spring, less in winter (ADR 022).
- **Loot** — what a march brings back, fixed at dispatch and never changed on the way, except by a recall. A forage's: for each resource the terrain yields, the lesser of units × rate × hours × the season's percent for that resource at dispatch, the units counted whatever their kind, floored once, and an even share of the carry, floored, the carry being the sum over the party of each kind's carry from content and never scaled by a season (ADR 021, ADR 022); the **partial loot** of a recalled march foraging is the same formula over the whole milliseconds foraged, at the season percents the march took at dispatch whatever season the recall falls in, never gold, and a march recalled outbound brings none. An attack's: the content's amount per point of the camp's strength fought, capped at the summed carry of the predicted survivors, split in even shares, floored, over the two resources the terrain yields and gold, the same in every season; nothing when the battle is lost (ADR 020). Credited to the stocks at the return even above the capacity, where they freeze, as a refund is (ADR 019).
- **Cargo** — what a transport carries from the fief it leaves to the lord's other fief: a whole amount of each resource, at least 1 in all and at most the party's carry, counted as the plain sum of its amounts, and taken from the stocks at dispatch. From the dispatch it is the **incoming cargo** of the fief it is sent to, at most one per fief, and that fief's resolve credits it at the arrival even above the capacity, where the stocks freeze. A recalled transport carries its cargo home: the other fief has none on its way from the recall instant, and the return credits it to the fief it left. Never *delivery*, which is a recruit order's (ADR 024).
- **March slot** — the fief's single place for the march away; one march at a time, no queue. A slot with a march away is **busy** (ADR 019).
- **Recall** — the lord turning a march back, named by its departure so a stale tab never recalls a newer march. Allowed while the march is outbound or foraging, so for an attack only before the battle (ADR 020), refused while returning (an instant on a boundary reads the later phase) and once it is home. Recalled outbound, the men walk back as long as they walked; recalled foraging, the full road back, as long as the dispatch fixed it; the partial loot is fixed at the recall, and the resolve credits it at the new return (ADR 019). A recall reads no season: the road back and the partial loot keep the percents of the dispatch (ADR 022). A founding is recalled only before its arrival: the reservation is lifted at the recall, and the settler walks home with no loot and is kept (ADR 023). A transport is recalled only before its arrival, and walks home with its cargo (ADR 024).
- **Camp** — a band of bandits holding a free plot; about the content's fraction of the free plots hold one, placed by a hash of the plot's coordinates, never a table, so a plot's camp is the same on every read. A camp has a tier and a strength, is shared by every lord, is attacked from the map, and is erased when a fief is founded on its plot (ADR 020), which only a lord's first fief can be: a founding is never sent to a camp's plot (ADR 023). Replaces the *expedition* encounter.
- **Tier** — a camp's size, 1, 2 or 3, drawn from the same hash and never changing; the content gives each tier a full strength and the hours it regrows in (ADR 020).
- **Strength** — the count that decides a battle. A unit's is its kind's from content, 1 for infantry, 2 for cavalry, 1 for the archer (ADR 025) and 0 for the settler, which never fights (ADR 023), and a party's is the sum of its units' (ADR 021); a camp's is its tier's full strength when never fought, and otherwise the strength its last battle left plus the regrowth since, the tier's full strength per its regrow hours, floored and capped at the full: derived on read, never advanced by a process (ADR 005, ADR 020).
- **Attack** — a march whose order is to fight the camp on its plot: sent to a camp's plot alone, refused on a plot without one, with no stay; the camp's strength is fixed at dispatch, so the outcome, the losses and the loot are read on the form before the men leave and nothing on the road changes them but a recall before the battle (ADR 020).
- **Battle** — the fight at an attack's arrival, deterministic from the count of each kind sent, their strengths summed and the camp's strength fixed at dispatch: the higher strength wins, a tie goes to the camp, the loser falls whole and the winner loses the rival's strength squared over its own, rounded up, always keeping at least one. A winning party's losses fall in the **loss order**, on the infantry first, then on the cavalry and last on the archers, and it keeps at least one unit in all (ADR 021, ADR 025). The dead leave the count, the camp's strength after the battle is recorded for every lord with the instant, and a won attack turns home with the survivors while a lost one ends there (ADR 020).
- **Scout** — the unit that reveals another fief. Replaces *espionage probe*. Not in this phase (W1).

## The chronicle

- **Chronicle** — a fief's record of its latest 100 events, written in the same act that applies them, the resolve, the cancel or the dispatch of a founding or of a transport; the domain never reads it (ADR 013, ADR 023, ADR 024).
- **Event** — one happening of one of twelve kinds: an upgrade finished, an art learned, an upgrade cancelled, a study cancelled, recruits delivered, recruits cancelled, a march returned, a battle fought, a founding sent, a fief founded, a transport sent, a transport arrived (ADR 024). It names its building or art and its level, or its unit and the count one recruit order delivered, or the delivered and the cancelled counts of a cancelled one, or the plot, the count of each kind, the loot and whether the march was recalled of a returned march, or the plot, the camp's tier, whether it was won, the count of each kind lost and the strength the camp lost of a battle, the refund for a cancel, and is stamped with the instant it happened: the finish for a finish, the cancel instant for a cancel, the last delivery for a recruit order, the return for a march, the arrival for a battle, never the instant of the read that wrote it. Every upgrade a cascade cancels is an event of its own, with its own refund; an upgrade the resolve drops from a restarted build queue is refunded without one; a recruit order is one event, never one per unit; a march is one event, at its return, never at its departure; an attack is a battle event at its arrival and, when won, a march returned at its return, two lines for one march (ADR 013, ADR 018, ADR 019, ADR 020, ADR 021). A founding sent and a fief founded each name the new fief and its plot: the first is the one event written at a dispatch, on the fief the settler leaves, since the plot is reserved from that instant; the second is stamped with the arrival and written on both chronicles, the new fief's first event. A founding recalled, or turned home at a held plot, writes a march returned at its return and no fief founded (ADR 023). A transport sent and a transport arrived each name the other fief, its plot and the cargo: the first is written at the dispatch on the fief the party leaves, since the stocks leave at that instant, the second on the fief that receives, stamped with the arrival. The party's return is a march returned on the fief it left, with no loot, or with the cargo as its loot when recalled, and a recalled transport writes no transport arrived (ADR 024).

## The account

- **Verified email** — a player's email once proven to receive mail, by following the verify link sent to it. It gates only a password reset, never sign-in or play; until then the signed-in shell shows a banner that resends the link (ADR 015).
- **Account token** — a single-use link token of kind `verify` or `reset`, mailed to a player and stored as a digest, never in clear. A verify token lives 24 hours, a reset token 1 hour; issuing one retires the player's earlier token of that kind, and one unknown, expired, used or of the other kind is refused as `TokenInvalid` (ADR 015).

## The return

- **Digest** — what happened on every fief of a player since their last acknowledgement: the chronicle events after it and the stores that filled after it, each with the instant it filled; due when there is something to tell and the acknowledgement is older than the content's absence; a card on the fief screen, never a modal, holding no resource snapshot (ADR 027).
- **Acknowledgement** — the instant a player last pressed *Entendido* on the digest, one per player, written by that act alone and never by a read; a new player's is the sign-up instant (ADR 027).
- **Goal** — the first entry of the content's ordered milestone list the fief has not met, a building at a level, met at the built level and evaluated by the server on read: underway while the slot or the queue holds the next level, otherwise pending with what the next level lacks (ADR 027).
- **Guidance** — the goal a fief shows its lord; dismissed for that fief forever by *Descartar*, stored on the fief, after which the fief answers no goal (ADR 027).
- **Hint** — one dismissible line shown the first time one of seven concepts matters, peasants, seasons, the queue, the library, the barracks, marches and a full store, at most one per screen in that order; a seen hint is stored per player and never shown again (ADR 027).

## Society

- **House** — a group of players under one banner. Replaces *alliance*. Not in this phase (W2).

## Time

- **Instant** — a point in time from the `Clock` port; the domain never reads the wall clock.
- **Duration** — a length of time in seconds; build times, study times, a unit's training and marches are durations. A build or study duration is fixed once, at the enqueue or the study start, from the content's seconds and the percents in force at that instant, the season's for both and the built library's for a study, rounded up to whole seconds in one step; it never changes while the work waits or runs (ADR 011, ADR 012, ADR 017). A unit's duration is fixed at the recruit order from the content's seconds, the built barracks level and the season's train percent in force at that instant, in one step, and never changes while the order runs (ADR 018, ADR 017 as amended). A march's road time is fixed at dispatch from the map's seconds, the road percent of the slowest kind sent and the season's road percent in force at that instant, in one step, and never changes while the march is away (ADR 019, ADR 021, ADR 022).
- **Season** — one of spring, summer, fall, winter, in force over the whole world at once for a fixed number of days set by the content's calendar, from its start, included, to the instant it ends, excluded. Each season scales every resource's rate by a whole percent from content, applied last (winter lowers food, spring raises it, fall raises gold), and scales build, study and train durations and the road of a march by a build, a study, a train and a road percent from content, fixed at the enqueue, the study start, the recruit order or the dispatch (summer shortens building, winter shortens study, spring shortens training, fall shortens the road); its resource percents also scale each resource of a forage loot, fixed at dispatch; there is no season before the calendar's epoch, and every percent reads 100 until then (ADR 016, ADR 017 as amended, ADR 022).
- **Year** — four seasons, counted from the first spring at the calendar's epoch: *año 1* is the first. There is no named calendar and no era (ADR 016).

## Learning

- **Word** — one entry of the **lexicon**, the curriculum's list of card words: its lemma, its part of speech, its example **frame** and that frame's gapped form, one gloss per scaffold language and an English **definition**, under an opaque id that never changes and is never the lemma. Its NGSL 1.2 rank is read from the NGSL file, never copied. A **frame word** is a closed-class word among the NGSL's first 100 ranks (*the*, *of*, *do*), listed in the grammar inventory alone, in the one structure that admits it, never in the lexicon and never a card; a **card word** is every other word, taught by exactly one lesson (ADR 035, ADR 037).
- **Grammar inventory** — `grammar.json`, the structures in curriculum order, each with its CEFR-J level, the frame words and the inflections it admits and the lesson that admits it; an inflection is a form of a frame word (*is*, *my*, *was*) or a card word's ending (*-s*, *-ed*, *-ing*, *-er*, *-est*). A frame uses no frame word or inflection its lesson does not admit (ADR 037, check 6).
- **Band** — one of three slices of the NGSL 1.2 by rank: 1 to 1 000, 1 001 to 2 000, 2 001 to 2 809. Progress, placement and the holdout are counted per band (ADR 037).
- **Frame** — the one English sentence that shows a card word in use, holding its lemma once in the lemma's own form and otherwise only frame words admitted by its lesson and card words of earlier lessons; its gapped form replaces the lemma with a blank for recall (ADR 036, ADR 037).
- **Lesson** — five new card words, five to nine in a band's last lesson, grouped by a theme, never by a semantic set, brought in through the school's lesson slot as a study is today, costing nothing; its words' cards enter at its finish, once per player; the school's built level caps how many words are in learning at once. Replaces nothing in OGame; its slot is the research lab's (ADR 035, ADR 036, ADR 037).
- **Chrome lesson** — one of the first lessons, which teach every card word the English chrome uses, the game nouns included, whatever their band; never placed out, and their words count in no measure (ADR 037, ADR 038).
- **Stage** — the lessons a player has studied or placed out of, in curriculum order; a text written for a lesson is shown only to a player whose stage reaches it (ADR 037).
- **Content gate** — the test over the shipped learning content, run by `pnpm gate`: the lexicon, the lessons' order, the frame rule, a running text's coverage, the glosses of every shipped scaffold, the grammar inventory and the skill curve; with the owner's review and merge of every gloss, frame, definition and text (ADR 037, ADR 039).
- **Placement** — the optional check at the first sign-in: per band, 30 words to recognise; a band passed at 29 of 30 is **placed out**, its lessons counted in the stage and still open to study (ADR 037).
- **Card** — one player's memory of one word, the player's and never the town's: its state (learning, review or relearning), its step, its stability, its difficulty, the instant and the rating of its last review, the instant it entered, and the instant its last review proved it retained, if it did. A hint or a near-miss waits on a **pending review** beside it until the rating. Its retrievability and its due instant are derived on read, never advanced by a process, by FSRS as ADR 036 pins it. Replaces nothing in OGame (ADR 035, ADR 036).
- **Review** — one answer to a due card, graded by the server: a **recognition** (choose the meaning among the lesson's words) while the card is learning, a **recall** (type the English from the gloss, with a hint on request) from then on. Rated Again when wrong, Hard when right after a hint or a near-miss, Good when right, never Easy; it updates the card, pays nothing, and is recorded in the **review log**, the append-only record of every review. Only a due card is reviewed. Replaces nothing in OGame (ADR 035, ADR 036).
- **Near-miss** — a typed answer one edit away (a letter added, dropped or changed, or two neighbours swapped) from a word of 4 letters or more, and no NGSL 1.2 word itself; it is not rated, but prompts one more answer (ADR 036).
- **Stability** — the days a card holds before its chance of recall falls to the desired retention. A 7-day stability is the floor a skill level needs; the unaided recall a week after the review before it is the other condition, under Skill (ADR 035, ADR 036).
- **Skill** — what the player knows at a level, earned by retention and never bought: a level is earned at the review after which every word of its lesson the player holds a card for, and at least one, stands at 7 days of stability or more and was last recalled unaided, rated Good, at least 7 days after the review before it; stored with that instant and never taken back. Every lesson of the curriculum earns one level, +5 %, of the resource its place in the **skill cycle** names, and nothing else earns one: not a review, not placement, not a released lesson. One resource's levels add up into one percent, as an art's levels do, so food at level 48 runs at 3.4 times its rate without skills, in every town the player holds. Replaces *art*, OGame's *technology* (ADR 035, ADR 036, ADR 039).
- **Skill cycle** — the game's resources in a fixed order, food first, that the curriculum's lessons raise in turn: lesson 1 food, lesson 2 the next, and so on round the cycle, so any run of as many lessons as there are resources raises each once (ADR 039).
- **Scaffold language** — the language a player picks to support the English: `es` or `none` at launch, a player field changed any time. Under `es` a word is supported by its Spanish gloss, under `none` by its English definition; the support fades with the word's stability. English is always the target, never a scaffold (ADR 035, ADR 038).
- **Chrome** — the copy of the game's screens. Under `es` it is Spanish until every chrome lesson is studied, both languages until their skills are earned, English after: the **chrome phase**, derived on read and never going back. **Fixed copy**, the account screens, refusals, review verdicts and mail, is always in the scaffold language (ADR 038).
- **Message catalog** — one ICU MessageFormat file per language with one key set, the single source of every string a client or a mail shows, generating the web's copy and Android's `strings.xml` (ADR 038).
- **Holdout** — the 10 % of a band's card words, outside the chrome lessons, drawn and stored per player when the player starts the band, never entered as cards and tested in the band's external check 8 and 24 weeks after its start against practised words of the same lessons, so practised against withheld is a comparison within one player; released after that band's 24-week check (ADR 035, ADR 037).

## The town

The terms of the rebuilt town, from ADR 040; each enters the code with its slice, beside the medieval term it replaces until the cutover.

- **Town** — the one holding a player develops, named by the player on the first signed-in visit and founded at the lowest free site; labelled **city** once its supplied people reach the content's count. Replaces *planet* and the fief (ADR 035, ADR 040).
- **People** — the town's workforce, derived and never stored: supplied by a base count and the homes, occupied by building levels and by hires; **free** or **at work** (ADR 007, ADR 040).
- **Building work** — the town's one build slot; the **waiting list** holds the work queued behind it, charged at the enqueue, refunded in full by a **stop** (ADR 011, ADR 040).
- **In learning** — a card whose stability is under 7 days; not the card's FSRS learning state, which lasts minutes. The **learning cap**, set by the school's level, bounds how many cards are in learning once a lesson's words enter, so a lesson start past it is refused (ADR 040).
- **Hire** — a worker or a truck, hired at the office in one hiring order at a time, occupying people and eating nothing. A **team** is the hires sent out together (ADR 018, ADR 021, ADR 040).
- **Job** — a team's trip to a free site for whole hours, coming back with **pay** fixed at dispatch from the site's terrain, never money; **called back** before it ends, it returns with the partial pay (ADR 019, ADR 040).
- **Waste site** — a site holding waste of one of three sizes, placed by a hash of its address and regrowing from its last clean-up. A **clean-up** is a team's trip to it: a team whose strength covers the waste cleans it and is paid money and material; a smaller team comes back with nothing; nobody is lost (ADR 020, ADR 040).
- **The Report** — the screen of the reviews due, then the Town News since the player last closed it (ADR 027, ADR 040).
- **Town News** — the town's events, each with its instant, written in the transaction that applies them (ADR 013, ADR 040).
- **Tip** — one dismissible line shown the first time a concept matters, seen tips stored per player. Replaces *hint* (ADR 027, ADR 040).
- **Cutover** — the last slice of the rebuild: it deletes the medieval code and drops the medieval tables, which is ADR 035's one-time reset (ADR 040).

## Avoided words

`planet`, `colony` (say new land, or a second fief), `colonist`, `colony ship` (say settler), `galaxy` (say kingdom), `galaxy view` (say kingdom map), `metal`, `crystal`, `deuterium`, `energy`, `fleet`, `ship`, `shipyard`, `troops`, `soldier` (say unit), `train queue` (the recruit slot has no queue), `mission`, `expedition`, `raid` (say attack for a march on a camp, march otherwise; no march meets another lord), `plunder` (say loot), `alliance`, `tech`, `technology`, `research` (say study), `mine` alone (say which one), `tick` (there is no tick), `population` (peasants are derived, never grown), `log` alone, `history`, `feed` (say chronicle; the review log is its own term), `confirmed email` (say verified email), `recovery` (say reset), `flashcard`, `deck` (say card), `quiz` (say review), `native language`, `L1` (say scaffold language), `XP`, `streak` (retention is the progress; ADR 028 refuses streaks).
